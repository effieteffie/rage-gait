Rage bait has become the leading mechanism and product of an attention economy that manufactures news and discourses for maximum traction. Our platform, rage gait, seeks out frameworks that regulate this frustration on an emotional level, and transform it into deliberate, actionable stances tailored to each reader’s abilities and needs.

Our usage of “gait” draws from Friedrich Engels’ 1876 essay, “The Part Played by Labour in the Transition from Ape to Man.” He argued that the “decisive step in the transition from ape to man” was adapting an “erect gait” that made walking without using one’s hands possible—thus freeing the hands to handle more complex tasks. So trivial to our current everyday lives yet so elementary to our evolution, the gait is a testament to how any momentum, no matter how small, can effect change so long as it is purposeful, intentional, and sustainable.

Following this principle, we reject weaponizing “rage” into an addictive affect and offering an anesthetic analysis that traps readers in an intellectually futile yet transactionally profitable loop. Instead, we situate the dystopian flashiness of technological novelty within the larger historical development of capitalism. Drawing on dialectical materialism, we provide frameworks, concepts, and comparisons to overcome the digital fetishism that paints our present as the end of history and our struggles as uniquely advanced.
