AI: /stage/ stage of capitalism #0

Just as a cozy sauna condenses into a suffocating stratum of hell, the warmer springs in Europe turn into unwavering heat waves.

“Artificial intelligence” is a confusing thing beyond the big numbers, eerie schemas, and the science fiction-ish vibe. Those, when stripped off their futuristic aura and computational sublime, are not that challenging to grasp. The discourses, myths, and politics that negate and constitute “AI”, on the other hand, are much harder to map out and decode. This is due to the simultaneous dispersion and density of AI as a phenomenon, which I think is its main inner contradiction.

It is dispersed in the sense that what AI means, what it entails, how and by whom it is operated are all sparse and ever-expanding discussions. Foremost, “AI” is built on a semantic uncertainty, as both the breast cancer-detecting software and the psychosis-inducing chatbots can be referred to as such. This shaky founding fragments the responsibility and accountability regarding AI’s development and deployment.

For instance, LLMs cannot be blamed for their racism, sexism or homophobia when they draw from preexisting human data, which is doomed to be biased. However, a corporation cannot be blamed for the lack of caution in their scraping and processing of that data if no firm restrictions were put into place. Moreover, states cannot be blamed for their insufficient governance of these companies, because restrictions only delay innovation for an invention that is so very urgent to perfect, lest [scary authoritarian totalitarian dictatorship] get there first.

But, despite the muddiness, AI is also incredibly dense and in-your-face: AI-slop and “AI-solutions” take over every trusted medium and application overnight, leading the charge to enshittification of culture. Serial layoffs are held over the heads of the white-collar workers, while AI bots regress their children’s cognitive development by the second. No matter how many times you vote “no” or go to press to stop a data center from getting built in your community, a surveilling and polluting neighbor moves next door anyway.

Such a duality of dispersion and density is characteristic of capitalism. Its intangible omniscience is how capital sustains and reproduces itself: An inner sense of worth and place in the world decided by various metrics and moments one cannot map out but are sure of. The vocalized identities and individualities that are isolated from one another as different struggles, concealing their shared origin exploitation. And the material reality all of it is built on — the distance between the worker who produces value, and the capitalist who obtains, mystifies, multiplies, and hoards the said value.

Right now, we live in the most extreme disparity of wealth, struggle, and consciousness — Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism, as Lenin coined. In the book, he mapped out the manners and mechanisms through which capitalist “free” markets inevitably birth monopolies, and how those monopolies further concentrate into “finance capital”, which is the most extreme abstraction of value from its origins, such as loans, stocks, and fictitious capital. Such wealth, too, concentrates in a gradually decreasing number of hands, who historically widened the gap between the worker and the capitalist — the most recent showcase being the making of the world’s first trillionaire, Elon Musk.

By the end of the text, Lenin exposes how imperialism’s inner contradictions are not only in spite of, but also for the ripening of the revolution: As enterprises grow larger and more central, more people are proletarianized to become part of and operate their machine. The more power the “rentier states” who live off by “clipping coupons” assume, the further they are abstracted from the means of productions they hoard. The less immediate their position is to the process, the more metaphysical their control over production becomes.

When combined, these peculiarities prove that the mechanisms needed to maximize profits and suppress the workers also risk providing them the knowledge, skill, and numbers to smash the bourgeois “shell”, and seize the very tools they’d optimized to socialize production. To delay this resolution, capitalism places plenty of tools in place. They all boil down to repressive state apparatus when investigated; but to keep a civilized front and to manufacture consent for their violent operations, imperialists employ ideological tools that distract and bribe the populations of the imperial core. They often work. So long as one’s immediate materiality is not impacted by a subject matter, they prioritize discoursal resolution over disruptive solidarity. Thus, condensed urgencies in the Global South get aestheticized into dispersed discourses in the Global North. A finger points at another, which dizzies anyone trying to follow the floaters; such scattered, loosely connected streaks of speech weave a cozy “common sense” anyone can seek shelter in when challenged.

What differentiates AI as a tool and a promise, though, if implemented aggressively, it might resolve the primary contradiction of imperialism Lenin diagnosed. AI can serve several functions to disempower the proletariat who are growing in number and skill: For instance, whether an AI tool actually works well or not, it only has to promise that it works well enough to replace the workers. This promise sells the “tool” immediately, which leads to layoffs that increase the competition and the enlisting to the “reserve army of labor”, while decreasing the wages and the number of workers. Also, the forced implementation of AI grows dependence on these devices and systems. This means that while the expertise of a worker deteriorates down to “manual labor” in every field, the proficiency required to run the means of production will be held by an exclusive group of people — challenging the possibility of a self-sustaining socialist body of government.

However, unlike previous instances that intricately construct narratives and seamlessly anesthetize the masses, AI followed a vile, gross, yet well-connected stream to bleed into our lives. I liken its internal particularities to water molecules to explain my point: A problematic phase of transition is crystallizing at every part of the world, prodding different pressure points of different people. Just as a cozy sauna condenses into a suffocating stratum from hell, the warmer springs in Europe turn into unwavering heat waves. In the case of the “white collar” in developing countries, those who rode the crisis and contradictions others couldn’t, the same water molecules sweat out of their pores just as their working hours extend and their conditions worsen — or just as job searches become their own marathons. Moreover, the same water floods the countries that bear the consequences of imperialism most violently — as seen in Nepal most recently — washing over lands, lives, and homes.

Whether it be in the form of destructive waves or blinding sweat drops, a certain aquatic form washes over the structures that held imperialism intact, and cleanses the lenses that obscured the scene. This is why I conceptualize AI as the /stage/ stage of capitalism. It is not a different play yet another act of imperialism — the most self-explanatory one yet. Within the digital, /stage/ indicates a disruption in the linguistic flow of code, a command that does not shape the syntax into the intended form, but only exposes its recipe and materials. In its content, /stage/ stage of capitalism exposes the depravity of this particular phenomena, as AI introduces neither the utopian futures nor concludes the dystopian destruction it promised — only leaving the audience with the void, washed out, and molding structure it is built on.

Finally, /stage/ stage also offers new possibilities. It removes the force and the code that keeps the audience in their seats. It forebodes an empty stage that can be redecorated, or entirely smashed to be rebuilt.

In AI's hollowness is its endless potential — so long as we do not passively observe and absorb, but articulate and counter its contradictions. We all hold unique agencies, spaces, and means to partake in this. Mine might be small and insignificant, maybe, but resistance is from each according to his ability, to each according to his needs.
